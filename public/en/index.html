Why this editor exists

For many hobby planners, Microsoft Publisher was the tool of choice: simple enough for quick network maps, without the overhead of InDesign or Affinity. It was discontinued at the end of October 2026.

There was no fitting replacement, so this editor was built to do exactly that job, by someone who plans transit networks as a hobby.

How a map comes together

  1. 1
    Describe the networkCreate stops, give lines a colour and category, assign the stops each line serves.
  2. 2
    Place the stopsSet them on the grid. The editor connects them with 0°, 45° and 90° segments and rounds the bends.
  3. 3
    ExportOutput as SVG, PNG or PDF. The project itself is saved as a JSON file on your computer.

What it can do

  • Lines with trunk, branches and loops
  • Parallel lines are bundled, lane order is adjustable
  • Split stops and waypoints
  • Direction-only stops, temporary service and pass-through stops
  • Key and line overview are generated automatically
  • Rivers, borders and areas as drawing objects, with labels
  • Text styles and designs you can save and reuse
  • Automatic label placement with overlap check
  • CSV import and background image as tracing template
  • Undo and redo, selection mode, export and import parts of a network

Free and local

The editor runs entirely in your browser. There is no sign-up and no server that sees your maps. Projects are saved as files on your computer.
